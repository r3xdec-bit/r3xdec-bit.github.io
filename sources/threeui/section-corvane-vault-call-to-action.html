Corvane Vault The charter sigil

Four charters are struck each winter — in vellum, in blackened iron, in oxblood and in bronze. Each is cut to a single hand, sealed at the deep vault, and honoured in every counting house from the Ember Coast to the Riven Gate. What is given to Corvane is not lent, not moved, and never forgotten.

The descent to the deep vault

Chapter I — The Approach

There is one door

Corvane keeps no branch and no counter at street level. There is one door, on a rock that floats, and the drop is asked to handle the rest.

Chapter II — The Counting Hall

Read aloud at dawn

Nine hundred tallies are read out each morning at the long desks, in a hall that has not missed a dawn in four hundred and seventy years.

Chapter III — The Long Stair

Four hundred and twelve steps

Cut into the underside by hands that wanted no lift, no shaft and no hurry. Every charter ever issued has been carried down them by a person who could be named.

Chapter IV — The Seal

A sigil, a name, a season

Two of the three can be forged by a patient hand. In four centuries the ring has never turned for a forgery, and we do not intend to learn why.

Chapter V — The Deep Vault

Older than the hand that set it down

Everything in the core has outlived the person who put it there. That is the whole promise, and it is the entire cost.

0 fathoms below the gate

Anatomy of a charter

Four marks that cannot be copied

Every charter leaves the striking room with the same four proofs on it. Three are cut by machine; the fourth is done by a person who has to be awake, sober and named in the day-book.

1 2 3 4
01

The guilloche

Nine hundred and four interlocking rosettes, cut on a rose engine that has turned since 1140. The gearing is changed every striking, so no two charters share a pass.

02

The crowned raven

Struck in leaf, never printed. Under a glass the gold still shows the hammer, and that is the only proof anyone at the Riven Gate will look at twice.

03

The blank field

Left bare on purpose. Your name goes here once, by the Keeper's own hand, in iron gall ink, on the morning you swear — and never again.

04

The bitten edge

Torn, not guillotined. The tear runs against a counterpart held in the deep vault. A charter that does not marry its counterpart is a charter that is not ours.

The four strikings

Choose the metal you will be known by

A house is not a tier of service — every charter draws on the same vault and the same oath. It is the company you keep at the Gate, and the door you are shown to when you arrive without warning.

House Vellum

For the scholar and the slow fortune. Struck on skin, kept out of the weather, and read more often than it is spent.

Admitted / yearForty

House Iron

For those who carry the ledger into open country. It has been through two fires and a river, and it still draws at the Gate.

Admitted / yearOne hundred

House Oxblood

For the old families and the older debts. Bound in hide, studded in brass, and generally inherited rather than requested.

Admitted / yearTwelve

House Bronze

For houses that intend to outlive their founders. Cast once, patinated on purpose, and settled only at the turn of a century.

Admitted / yearFour

Entered by hand, witnessed by two

Terms of keeping

What it costs to be remembered

Fees are levied once a year at the frost moon and never in arrears. There is no penalty for withdrawal, because there has never been a case of it.

The Lesser Charter

6marks / year
  • House Vellum striking, winter issue
  • Drawing rights at nine counting houses
  • Quarterly tally, read and sealed
  • One named successor

  • Successors unlimited, in perpetuity
