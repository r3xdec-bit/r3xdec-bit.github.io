Two faces

By day, the island is a sanctuary. By night, the mountain remembers.

Kairo rose from the southern ocean without a name, four hundred miles from the nearest chart. It is private, unhurried and never quite still. Guests share it with no one else.

  • Twelve guestsper sailing, and no one else on the island
  • Three fallsthat leave the rim and never reach the sea
  • One mountainthat glows on the nights it chooses to
The passage

Reached by sea, and only by sea.

  1. Day 1 · Dusk

    Depart Port Sable

    The expedition vessel Meridian leaves the harbour with twelve guests and a crew of nine. Dinner is served as the coast goes dark.

  2. Day 2 · Open water

    Thirty-six hours south

    A long swell, then the cloud shelf. You will see the summit before you see the sea beneath it.

  3. Day 3 · Before dawn

    Landfall

    A tender to the lee cove, a cable-car cut into the cliff, and coffee on the terrace as the falls catch first light.

Hour thirty-four

The summit, before the sea beneath it.

The stay

Six suites. One island. Nothing ordinary.

The suites are cut into the leeward cliff, two guests each, with basalt walls, wool, linen and glass that opens onto the falls. One long table, one cook, no signal and no schedule.

The expedition

Four sailings a year. Twelve places each.

Thirty-six hours aboard the Meridian

Sailings leave Port Sable on Friday at dusk. The passage is part of the expedition: cabins for twelve, a galley crew of three, and an open bridge for anyone who wants the night watch.

Guided exploration

Two island guides walk the summit route at dawn, the passage to the thermal chamber by lamplight, and the rim path where the falls leave the island. Every walk is optional; every walk is small.
