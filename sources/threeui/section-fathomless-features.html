Midnight

A thousand metres down, the last daylight has been gone for an hour. Everything that shines here had to make the light itself.

Nothing down here is still

Water pushes in long slow swells instead of gusts, so a kelp stipe leans for whole seconds at a time and the snow falling through it never quite settles. Drag a cursor across the column and the water answers — motes part, close behind you, and drift back into the current a moment later.

Marine snow

A continuous fall of shed shell, moulted skin and broken drift, sinking somewhere near a hundred metres a day. Almost everything living below the light is eating it, or eating something that did.

02 / 0–40 m

The stipe is not a trunk

It carries no water and lays down no rings. It grows toward the surface, holds on with a tangle rather than a root, and lets the swell bend it flat twice a minute without ever breaking.

03 / 600 m–

Counter-illumination

Some animals light their own undersides to erase the silhouette they would otherwise cast against the surface. Cover the light and they reappear; cover the animal and the light carries on.
